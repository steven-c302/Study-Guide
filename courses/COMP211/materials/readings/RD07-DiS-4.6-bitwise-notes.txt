RD07 reading notes - Dive into Systems 4.6 (bitwise operators and shifts) - study notes written for this guide
Bitwise operators work on the individual bits of integers; logical operators work on truth values. AND (&) gives 1 only where both bits are 1; OR (|) where at least one bit is 1; XOR (^) where exactly one bit is 1; NOT (~) flips every bit. Example: 0b011010 & 0b110110 = 0b010010; | = 0b111110; ^ = 0b101100; ~0b011010 flips all bits.
Shifts move bits by a number of places. Left shift fills the right with zeros and usually grows the value (multiplies by 2^k), but bits shifted past the end are lost, which can make it smaller (0b00101101 << 2 = 0b10110100 in 8 bits). Right shift discards bits that fall off the right. Whether zeros or ones enter on the left depends on whether the number is signed or unsigned: a logical right shift fills with zeros, an arithmetic right shift copies the leftmost (sign) bit.
